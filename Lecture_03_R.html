Time Series Features

A time series is a sequence of data points recorded or measured in chronological order, usually at regular, equally spaced intervals.

  • Trend: A trend exists when there is a long-term increase or decrease in the data.

  • Seasonality: Regular patterns that repeat over fixed periods like days, months, or years.

  • Cyclic: Long-term ups and downs that do not follow a fixed seasonal schedule.

  • Irregular: Random, unpredictable short-term changes

Time plot

For time series data, the obvious graph to start with is a time plot. That is, the observations are plotted against the time of observation, with consecutive observations joined by straight lines.

Box-Cox Transformation

Box-Cox transformations includes logarithmic and power transformations controlled by \(\lambda\)

Definition

\[ \boxed{ w_t = \begin{cases} \log(y_t), & \lambda = 0, \\[4pt] \dfrac{ \operatorname{sign}(y_t)\lvert y_t\rvert^\lambda - 1 }{ \lambda }, & \lambda \neq 0. \end{cases} } \]

This modified formulation allows negative values of \(y_t\), provided that

\[ \lambda > 0. \]

Role of \(\lambda\)

\[ \begin{array}{ccl} \lambda = 1 &\Rightarrow& w_t = y_t - 1 \\[3pt] \lambda = 0 &\Rightarrow& w_t = \log(y_t) \\[3pt] \lambda \neq 0 &\Rightarrow& \text{power transformation} \end{array} \]

  • \(\lambda = 1\) changes only the level

  • \(\lambda = 0\) applies a log transformation

  • \(\lambda \neq 0\) change the shape and scale

Choose \(\lambda\) to stabilize the variance, reduce scale-dependent fluctuations, and make the variation more consistent over time.

Box & Cox (1964); modified transformation from Bickel & Doksum (1981).

Technical Indicators

The simple moving average assigns equal weight to the \(w\) most recent observations:

\[ \operatorname{MA}_{t}^{(w)} = \frac{1}{w} \sum_{j=0}^{w-1}Y_{t-j}. \]

The exponential moving average assigns greater weight to more recent observations:

\[ \operatorname{EMA}_{t} = \alpha Y_t + (1-\alpha)\operatorname{EMA}_{t-1}, \qquad 0<\alpha\leq1. \]

The same weighting principles can be applied to other local measures. Moving means track changes in local position, while moving standard deviations track changes in local dispersion.

Time Series Decomposition

A time series \(Y_t\) might be decomposed into three additive components

\[ Y_t = T_t +S_t + R_t \] where \(T_t\), \(S_t\), and \(R_t\) are the trend, seasonal and the remainder, respectively.

Let \(m\) denote the seasonal period. The components can be estimated as follows:

  1. Trend: \(\hat{T}_t = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{j=-\lfloor m/2 \rfloor}^{\lfloor m/2 \rfloor} Y_{t+j}\), where a centered moving average smooths short-term and seasonal fluctuations.

  2. Detrended series: \(D_t = Y_t-\widehat{T}_t.\) For each seasonal position \(k=1,\ldots,m\).

  3. Seasonal component:\(\widetilde{S}_k = \frac{1}{n_k} \sum_{t:\,s(t)=k} D_t\), and centering the resulting seasonal indices: \(\widehat{S}_k = \widetilde{S}_k - \frac{1}{m} \sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\widetilde{S}_{\ell}, \qquad \sum_{k=1}^{m}\widehat{S}_k=0.\)

  4. Remainder: \(\widehat{R}_t = Y_t-\widehat{T}_t-\widehat{S}_{s(t)}\)

Time Series Decomposition

A trend exists when there is a long-term increase or decrease in the data. It does not have to be linear. Sometimes we will refer to a trend as “changing direction”, when it might go from an increasing trend to a decreasing trend.

A seasonal pattern occurs when a time series is affected by seasonal factors such as the time of the year or the day of the week. Seasonality is always of a fixed and known period.

A cycle occurs when the data exhibit rises and falls that are not of a fixed frequency. These fluctuations are usually due to economic conditions, and are often related to the “business cycle”. The duration of these fluctuations is usually at least 2 years.

Time Series Features

Trend strength

Trend strength compares the remainder with the trend component:

\[ F_T = \max\left( 0,\, 1 - \frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_t)} {\operatorname{Var}(T_t + R_t)} \right). \]

  • \(F_T \approx 0\): little or no trend
  • \(F_T \approx 1\): strong trend

Seasonal strength

Seasonal strength compares the remainder with the detrended series:

\[ F_S = \max\left( 0,\, 1 - \frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_t)} {\operatorname{Var}(S_t + R_t)} \right) \]

  • \(F_S \approx 0\): little or no seasonality
  • \(F_S \approx 1\): strong seasonality

Both measures range from 0 to 1. Values closer to 1 indicate that the corresponding systematic component explains substantially more variation than the remainder.
