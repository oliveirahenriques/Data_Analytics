What can a time coordinate represent?

  • Timestamp: a specific instant.
  • Fixed period: a span such as a month or quarter.
  • Interval: a start and an end.
  • Elapsed time: distance from an origin.

The representation must match what the observation actually measures.

Use preço de fechamento como exemplo de timestamp e produção mensal como período. Explique que a escolha altera slicing, agregação e labels.

[Sources] - https://wesmckinney.com/book/time-series

Fixed-frequency and irregular series

Fixed frequency

  • every 15 seconds;
  • hourly;
  • business daily;
  • monthly.

Irregular

  • trades;
  • incidents;
  • publications;
  • event records.

A line can display either, but lag 1 has a stable duration only under a regular calendar.

Em série irregular, shift(1) significa registro anterior, não necessariamente uma hora anterior.

Time Series Patterns

Four pattern concepts

Pattern Working definition
Trend long-term increase or decrease; not necessarily linear
Seasonality repeating pattern with a fixed, known period
Cycle rise and fall without a fixed, known frequency
Irregular remaining variation not explained by visible structure

A diferença principal entre sazonalidade e ciclo é a periodicidade fixa e conhecida da sazonalidade. Tempo: 4 min.

Trend is scale-dependent

A janela de 30 dias é uma escolha analítica. Suavização remove detalhes e introduz perdas nas bordas. Tempo: 3 min.

Seasonality has a known period

Hourly data may contain several seasonal periods:

  • daily: 24 observations;
  • weekly: 24 × 7 observations;
  • annual: calendar-dependent for hourly data.

A series can contain more than one seasonal pattern.

Período é expresso em observações e depende da frequência. Em calendários reais, horário de verão e anos bissextos exigem cuidado. Tempo: 3 min.

Cycles are not seasons

A cycle:

  • may last several years or varying durations;
  • does not repeat at a fixed known interval;
  • can appear as a trend in a short sample;
  • should not be inferred from one rise and fall alone.

Destaque dependência da janela observada. Um trecho curto de ciclo pode parecer tendência. Tempo: 2 min.

Checkpoint 2: name the evidence

For each description, choose trend, seasonality, cycle, irregular variation, or insufficient evidence:

  1. demand peaks at similar hours every day;
  2. a 60-day sample declines continuously;
  3. variance grows with the level;

  5. one isolated spike occurs;

Lag Plots

A lag is a shifted observation

For lag k:

  • vertical axis: current value, y_t;
  • horizontal axis: previous value, y_{t-k};
  • points close to the diagonal indicate persistence;
  • structures at seasonal lags suggest seasonal dependence.

Lag deve ser interpretado em unidades da frequência. Em dados horários, k=24 representa um dia. Tempo: 3 min.

Autocorrelation

From lag plots to a summary coefficient

Autocorrelation at lag k measures linear association between y_t and y_{t-k}.

r_k =
\frac{\sum_{t=k+1}^{T}(y_t-\bar{y})(y_{t-k}-\bar{y})}
{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(y_t-\bar{y})^2}

The collection of coefficients is the autocorrelation function, or ACF.

Explique numerador como covariação defasada e denominador como escala total. ACF resume relação linear, não toda dependência possível. Tempo: 4 min.

Reading common ACF shapes

  • slow decay: possible trend or long persistence;
  • repeated peaks: seasonal dependence;
  • alternating signs: oscillating dynamics;
  • isolated small spikes: sampling variability may be sufficient;
  • near-zero coefficients: behavior compatible with white noise, subject to uncertainty.

Fale em evidências compatíveis, não diagnósticos definitivos. Tempo: 3 min.

White Noise

What is white noise?

A white-noise series has:

  • no systematic autocorrelation;
  • approximately constant mean and variance under the usual simple definition;
  • unpredictable observations from its own past linear structure.

White noise is a benchmark for “no remaining temporal signal.”

Não confunda white noise com qualquer série aleatória ou com erros necessariamente normais. Tempo: 3 min.

Approximate reference bounds

For a white-noise series of length T, a common approximate reference is:

\pm \frac{1.96}{\sqrt{T}}
white_noise_bound = 1.96 / np.sqrt(len(white_noise))

Checkpoint 5: white-noise reasoning

Which statement is defensible?

  1. every ACF spike must equal zero;
  2. one spike outside a bound proves predictability;
  3. no visible time-plot pattern proves white noise;
  4. white noise provides a benchmark for residual diagnostics;
  5. normally distributed observations are necessarily white noise.

A quarta é a afirmação defensável. Normalidade marginal não implica independência temporal. Tempo: 3 min.

  2. presents a full and a zoomed time plot;
  3. identifies at least two temporal patterns;
  4. uses one seasonal plot and one subseries or heatmap;
  5. examines temperature as a covariate;
  6. compares lag 1, lag 24, and lag 168;
  7. creates an ACF plot;
  8. compares the result with white noise;
  9. states three forecasting implications;
  10. states three limitations.

Key Takeaways

Time-series visualization checklist

Transforme em checklist permanente para projetos de previsão. Tempo: 3 min.

Main conclusions

  1. Plot the data before selecting a forecasting method.
  2. Time-index quality determines the validity of temporal graphics.
  3. Viewing window and aggregation change visible structure.
  4. Seasonal plots reorganize time to expose repetition.
  5. Scatterplots reveal relationships, but not causality.
  6. Lag plots preserve shape; ACF compresses linear dependence.
  7. White noise is a benchmark, not a visual certainty.
  8. No single chart provides a complete temporal diagnosis.
  9. Visual evidence should become explicit, testable forecasting hypotheses.
